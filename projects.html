SEPIC Power Converter

Industry · AnySignal

Power Converter designed for S-Band, 20 or 30-Watt High Power Amplifier product for AnySignal. Converter at 32V, 3A has 85% efficiency with 1% voltage ripple. Single-Ended Primary Inductor Converter (SEPIC) was chosen as the topology due to it being a buck-boost as well as its simplicity with magnetics. A low pass LC filter was also added to the end of the converter to help with SEPIC’s inherent higher voltage ripple. LM5122 was chosen as a controller due to similarities to the LM5022, a controller on which we have radiation data on. The main difference between the two controllers is LM5122 has both a LO and HO output, meaning that a MOSFET can be used instead of the rectifying diode in a typical SEPIC converter. All other parts are rated to 30% of the current as per EEE-INST-002. Main challenges in design were mitigating ripple as well as heat dissipation from the primary switching MOSFET. The ripple was improved through placing an LC low pass filter at the end of the circuit. The heat fro the MOSFET was reduced by increasing V_GS due to this pushing the MOSFET to a more efficient operation mode.

  • Power105 W
  • Efficiency85% at 3A
  • Ripple1% voltage ripple
  • Tools LT Spice, Keysight ADS PE-Pro, TI Power Stage Designer

IEEE WRAP Hardware:RF Transceiver Front-End

Course Project

This project was part of IEEE at UCLA's Wireless, RF, and Analog Project (WRAP). I designed a complete transistor-level RF transceiver front-end that successfully transmitted and received BPSK symbols. I simulated the transceiver blocks in LTspice and used Keysight ADS for impedance matching to maximize power transfer. The project also included an R&D phase in which the amplifiers, mixers, and oscillators were redesigned as differential circuits to improve SNR and reduce loss.
As project lead, I also held lectures where I taught WRAP students (mostly third and second year undergraduates) about basic RF circuit design, including lectures on RF test and measurement, class A amplifiers,mixers, oscillators, impedance matching, and other RF basics. Then, I coordinated the RnD phase of the project where 5 students worked on redesigning the transceriver blocks as differential circuits.

  • ModulationBPSK
  • LevelTransistor-level design
  • ToolsLTspice, Keysight ADS

Integrated Microwave Power Combiner

Research · High Speed Electronics Lab

Designed in UCLA High Speed Electronics Lab (P.I. M.C. Frank Chang). Achieved 0.6dB insertion loss across 1 GHz passband at 180 GHz. The design process began with designing the the individual coupled inductors for the power converter using lumped circuit elements in order to accurately estimate the expected passband for the transformers. By properly accounting for parasitic capacitance, resistance, and inductance, both the bandwidth as well as passband frequency can be chosen. From the lumped circuit model, the individual inductor layout shape was designed, and from there combined to make the power combiner. The main challenges for this project were the trade-offs between inductance, capacitance, and resistance within the combiner. Resistance means loss, so to get less resistance, there must be wider traces, which means more capacitance, which affects the frequency response of the combiner.

  • D-band loss0.6 dB at 114 GHz
  • Process28nm CMOS
  • ToolsKeysight ADS, EM co-simulation

RF Power Amplifier Test Automation

Industry · AnySignal

Developed automated test scripts for RF power amplifiers using PyVISA and SciPy, running on a USRP B-200 with GNU Radio. Performed large-signal characterization on PA candidates for a 20–30 watt high-power-amplifier product line during development. Also, performed load-pull tuning on PA candidates for efficiency, linearity, and gain.

  • Target20–30W HPA candidates
  • HardwareUSRP B-200
  • ToolsGNU Radio, PyVISA, SciPy
